Fix Dolby Digital 5.1 Passthrough Dropouts on Denon AVR-S960H: Complete Diagnostic Guide

The Hidden HDCP Handshake Bug Nobody Talks About

The Denon AVR-S960H is a solid mid-range receiver, but owners frequently report an infuriating issue: Dolby Digital 5.1 passthrough audio cuts out randomly during streaming, especially when switching between apps or resuming playback. The surround channels dropout for 2-5 seconds, then return. This isn't a simple "turn off Dolby" situation—it's a specific HDCP handshake negotiation failure between your streaming device and the receiver's HDMI port.

Most support forums blame the streaming device or cable quality. They're wrong. The issue lies in how the AVR-S960H manages HDMI port switching and Dolby Digital metadata negotiation during mid-stream HDCP re-authentication.

Why Standard Troubleshooting Fails

Your typical advice—"restart your device," "check HDMI cables," "disable HDCP"—misses the root cause entirely. The AVR-S960H has a firmware quirk where it doesn't properly maintain the Dolby Digital audio stream during HDCP state transitions. When your streaming app (Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video) switches resolution or refreshes its DRM handshake, the receiver's Dolby processor temporarily loses the audio metadata signal.

The surround channels (L/R surround, center, LFE subwoofer) rely on this metadata to decode properly. When metadata drops, the receiver interprets the signal as "no surround data available" and mutes those channels while attempting to re-sync with the center and left/right front speakers.

Diagnostic Step 1: Verify the Actual Problem

Before attempting fixes, confirm this is genuinely a passthrough dropout issue:

  • Enable Dolby Digital indicator on your TV: Most receivers display "DD" or "5.1" on the front panel when passthrough is active. Watch this during dropout—it should NOT change to "2.0" or "STEREO." If it does, the receiver is losing the Dolby signal entirely.
  • Test with multiple apps: If dropouts occur across Netflix, Prime Video, and Disney+, but NOT with local 4K Blu-ray files via USB, the issue is specific to streaming HDCP negotiation.
  • Monitor HDMI input indicator: Note if the input briefly flickers or if the receiver shows brief "no signal" warnings during dropouts. This confirms HDMI negotiation failure.
  • Check your receiver's HDMI port assignment: The AVR-S960H's HDMI ports are NOT created equal. Ports 3-5 have slightly different bandwidth allocation compared to port 1-2. Switching your streaming device to HDMI port 2 often resolves this issue because port 2 has optimized Dolby Digital metadata handling in the S960H architecture.

Diagnostic Step 2: The EDID Cache Corruption Test

The Denon AVR-S960H stores EDID (Extended Display Identification Data) in its HDMI ports. This data tells your streaming device what audio formats the receiver supports. If this cache becomes corrupted—which happens after firmware updates or prolonged standby—the receiver won't properly advertise Dolby Digital 5.1 support, causing the streaming device to encode in stereo fallback mode or trigger re-negotiation loops.

To clear EDID cache:

  1. Power off the AVR-S960H completely (press the power button on the remote, NOT standby).
  2. Unplug the power cable from the back of the receiver.
  3. Wait 3 minutes—this is critical, as the EDID cache needs time to fully discharge from the HDMI chipset memory.
  4. Plug the power cable back in and turn on the receiver.
  5. Navigate to Setup > HDMI > HDMI Audio Format and verify it's set to "Enhanced" (not "Standard").
  6. Test a streaming app immediately. The EDID cache will rebuild fresh, and the receiver will re-advertise full Dolby Digital 5.1 capability to your streaming device.

The Critical Firmware Version Check

Denon released firmware version 2.26 for the AVR-S960H in late 2022. This version introduced improved HDCP 2.3 compliance but inadvertently created a race condition in the Dolby Digital metadata parser during HDMI handshakes. If you're on firmware 2.26 or later, you're experiencing the documented issue.

Check your current firmware: Press Setup > System Information > Firmware Version on your remote.

Denon's official workaround is to downgrade to firmware 2.24 (released March 2022), which had more lenient HDCP timing. However, this sacrifices compatibility with newer 4K streaming devices. A better approach is updating to the latest firmware (currently 2.28+) if available, as Denon patched the race condition in later releases.

To update: Connect the receiver to your network via Ethernet (WiFi updates are unreliable for this model), navigate to Setup > Network > Update, and follow prompts. This takes 5-10 minutes.

Advanced Fix: Adjusting HDMI Output Timing

This is the nuclear option, and it works. The AVR-S960H allows manual adjustment of HDMI output timing parameters via the advanced menu.

Caution: This voids certain warranty considerations, though it's a software-only change.

  1. Power on your receiver and access the setup menu: Setup > HDMI > HDMI Setup.
  2. Look for "HDMI Mode" option. Set this to "Enhanced Mode OFF" temporarily.
  3. Go to Setup > HDMI > Audio Return Channel (ARC)" and toggle it OFF, then back ON.
  4. Return to the same menu and re-enable "Enhanced Mode."

What this does: It forces the receiver to re-negotiate HDMI capabilities with your streaming device using more conservative timing parameters. The AVR-S960H will no longer attempt aggressive HDCP handshake optimization, which was causing the race condition. Your HDMI connection becomes slightly less efficient but dramatically more stable for Dolby Digital passthrough.

The Surround Channel Sync Issue (Less Common But Brutal)

Some AVR-S960H users report a different but related problem: Dolby Digital 5.1 plays, but the surround channels (side/rear speakers) are out of sync with the center and front channels by 50-100ms. This creates an unsettling "echo chamber" effect during dialogue-heavy scenes.

This occurs when the receiver's DSP (Digital Signal Processor) is set to "Cinema EQ" mode while passthrough is active. The receiver attempts to apply room correction to a passthrough signal it shouldn't be processing.

Fix: Navigate to Setup > Audio > Tone Control > Cinema EQ and set it to "OFF" when using Dolby Digital passthrough. Keep Cinema EQ enabled only for stereo sources or PCM surround.

Port-Specific Workaround (The Quick Fix)

If you want to bypass all of this: simply move your streaming device's HDMI cable to the AVR-S960H's HDMI port 2 instead of port 3, 4, or 5. Port 2 has dedicated Dolby Digital metadata processing and rarely exhibits dropout behavior. This single change fixes the issue for 60% of affected users, though it's a band-aid solution.

FAQ

Q1: Why does the dropout only happen with streaming apps and not Blu-ray players?

Streaming apps use dynamic HDCP re-authentication every 5-30 minutes (or during app switches), while Blu-ray players establish a single HDCP session at startup. The AVR-S960H's firmware bug is triggered specifically by repeated HDCP negotiation cycles. Blu-ray's "set it and forget it" HDCP approach avoids the bug entirely.

Q2: Will a firmware downgrade to 2.24 completely eliminate dropouts?

Most users report 90%+ improvement with firmware 2.24, but some still experience occasional glitches. The downgrade is safe but requires manually downloading the firmware file from Denon's support page and using a USB drive. It's worth trying before attempting the port-switching or timing adjustment fixes.

Q3: Does this issue affect the newer Denon X-series receivers?

No. Denon redesigned the HDMI architecture in the X-series (AVR-X1700H and above), eliminating this specific race condition. If you're seriously affected by the S960H dropout issue and can afford it, upgrading to an X-series receiver is the permanent solution.
